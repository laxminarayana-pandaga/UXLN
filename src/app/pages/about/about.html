AI & Design

AI

I've been exploring the broader AI landscape seriously — not as a curiosity, but as a working tool inside my design practice.

AI has changed what's possible in the first hours of a design sprint. Rapid concept generation, prototype exploration, research synthesis, edge-case stress testing — work that used to take days can happen in hours. That compression is genuinely significant. It means more time for the parts of design that require actual judgment: defining what the problem is, deciding what to prioritize, and making calls that AI can't make for you.

I use AI-augmented workflows in Figma, Claude and GPT for ideation and content synthesis, and generative tools for rapid visual exploration. I also work closely with engineering teams building AI-enabled product features, which means I understand both how these systems work and where they fail.

AI is a capability multiplier for a designer who already knows what good looks like. It's not a substitute for that knowledge.
